Neural bases of discourse reference

We investigate neural mechanisms supporting the maintenance and retrieval of discourse representations, with an emphasis on the interaction of referential form, discourse context, and memory demands. ERP and fMRI studies examine responses to names and pronouns in sentence and discourse contexts, the representation of plural discourse entities, and the contribution of spatial brain systems to personal-reference tracking.

Current research uses transcranial direct current stimulation to examine the contribution of superior parietal cortex to reference processing during reading comprehension.
